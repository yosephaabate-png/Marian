Gram Staining Procedure

Developed by Hans Christian Gram (1884). Differentiates bacteria based on cell wall structure.

StepAgentPurpose
1Crystal violetPrimary stain - colours all cells purple
2Iodine mordantForms crystal violet-iodine complex (locks dye)
3Acetone/alcoholDecolouriser - washes Gram- cells
4SafraninCounterstain - colours Gram- cells pink/red

Gram-positive: Thick peptidoglycan (20-80 nm) retains crystal violet-iodine complex even after acetone wash. Result: PURPLE.

Gram-negative: Thin peptidoglycan (2-7 nm) + outer membrane. Acetone dissolves outer membrane lipids, releasing dye. Safranin counterstain = PINK.

Clinical importance: Guides antibiotic choice. Gram+ infections respond to penicillins; Gram- require drugs that penetrate outer membrane.

Mechanism of beta-lactam resistance: Bacteria produce beta-lactamase enzymes that cleave the beta-lactam ring. ESBLs (extended-spectrum beta-lactamases) are a major concern in Ethiopian hospitals.

Ethiopian Antimicrobial Resistance

2021 BMC Infectious Diseases Study (Ethiopia)
Multi-centre surveillance across Ethiopian hospitals revealed alarming resistance patterns.
  • E. coli: Greater than 80% resistance to ampicillin. High resistance to co-trimoxazole and tetracycline. Carbapenems still mostly effective but ESBL producers increasing.
  • MRSA: Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus prevalence 20-35% in Ethiopian hospitals. Vancomycin remains active but access is limited.
  • S. typhi: Fluoroquinolone resistance emerging. Multidrug-resistant (MDR) typhoid a growing concern in Ethiopian urban areas.
  • Drivers of AMR in Ethiopia: Unregulated antibiotic sales (no prescription required in many pharmacies), incomplete treatment courses, poor infection control in healthcare settings, agricultural antibiotic use.
  • Policy response: Ethiopia National Action Plan on AMR (2018-2022). WHO GLASS surveillance programme. One Health approach linking human, animal, and environmental sectors.
  • Impact: AMR estimated to cost Ethiopia USD 1.5 billion annually in lost productivity and healthcare costs (WHO estimate).
